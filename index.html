More than food.
It's our heritage.

CALABARZON brings together five provinces, each with its own beloved delicacies and food traditions. From savory noodles to sweet coconut pastries and rice cakes, these local flavors offer a delicious way to experience Filipino culture.

Mission

To showcase CALABARZON’s diverse cuisine, preserve culinary traditions, promote local delicacies, and introduce the region’s authentic flavors to the world.

Vision

To become a global platform celebrating CALABARZON’s culinary heritage, inspiring appreciation for its local flavors, traditions, and Filipino cultural identity.

Cavite
01 / COASTAL CLASSIC

Pancit Pusit

A signature dish from Cavite City, this striking noodle dish gets its deep black color from squid ink. Garlic, onion, and seasonings build its savory seafood flavor, often finished with chicharon and herbs.

Food story
Rooted in Cavite's coastal food culture.
Where to try
Local eateries in Cavite City, including Asiong’s Carinderia.

Laguna
02 / SWEET FAVORITE

Buko Pie

This much-loved pastry from Los Baños pairs a golden, flaky crust with a milky filling packed with tender young coconut meat. It became a popular pasalubong and a sweet taste of Laguna.

Food story
Popularized by the Pahud sisters in Los Baños.
Where to try
Pasalubong shops, native bakeries, and roadside stops around Laguna.

Batangas
03 / COMFORT IN A BOWL

Batangas Lomi

Thick, comforting soup made with fresh miki noodles in a rich pork broth. It is commonly topped with ingredients such as kikiam, pork meatballs, liver, egg, and spring onion.

Food story
Associated with Lipa City and the story of To Kim Eng and Lipa Panciteria.
Where to try
Lomi houses in Lipa City and nearby towns.

Rizal
04 / TRADITIONAL KAKANIN

Suman

A classic Filipino rice cake made from glutinous rice, steamed or cooked and wrapped in leaves. Its texture is sticky yet soft, with a gentle flavor that can be sweet or unsweetened.

Food story
A traditional rice delicacy with roots in pre-colonial food traditions.
Where to try
Public markets, kakanin stalls, bakeries, and local fiestas.

Quezon
05 / A LOCAL EXPERIENCE

Pancit Habhab

Lucban's famous stir-fried noodles combine miki Lucban with pork, shrimp, and vegetables. Served on banana leaves with vinegar, it is traditionally eaten without utensils. The word “habhab” refers to eating directly with the mouth.

Food story
A well-loved merienda and street food, especially during the Pahiyas Festival.
Where to try
Lucban, Quezon, including Old Center Panciteria.
